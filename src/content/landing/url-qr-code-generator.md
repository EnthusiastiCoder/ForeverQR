A URL QR code is the most common kind: someone points a phone camera at it and their browser opens a web page. Type or paste your address into the studio above and the code appears as you type. Nothing is uploaded and there is no account to create.

## How to make one

1. Paste the full address, including the https:// part. If you leave it off, the studio adds it for you.
2. Check the preview. The scan badge tells you whether the code reads reliably, and lists the reason when it does not.
3. Pick colours and shapes if you like, or keep the plain black and white default, which is the safest choice for scanning.
4. Download an SVG for print, a PNG for screens, or a PDF sized for a page.

## Why this code never expires

Many websites that offer free QR codes quietly make "dynamic" ones. The square contains a short link on their server, which forwards to your page. It looks the same, but the moment their free trial ends, your printed code goes dead. ForeverQR does not do that. The square encodes your address itself, so the only thing that can break it is your own page going away.

If you are unsure about a code someone else made, the [expiry checker](/check-if-qr-code-will-expire/) tells you whether it goes through a middleman.

## Tips for a code that scans well

- Keep the address short. Fewer characters means a simpler, larger-patterned code that is easier to read from a distance.
- Leave the blank border (the quiet zone) around the code. Cutting it off is the most common reason prints fail.
- Keep strong contrast between the dots and the background, and dark dots on a light background.
- Print it at least two centimetres wide, and larger if people will scan from further away. A good rule is one tenth of the scanning distance.
- Test the printed result with a couple of different phones before you order a thousand copies.

## Where people use them

Menus, posters, business cards, product packaging, event badges, shop windows and slide decks all work well. For a permanent sign or a packaging run, a static code is the sensible choice precisely because you cannot afford for it to lapse.

## What happens to your data

Everything happens in your browser. The address you type is turned into the code on your own device, and the site makes no requests to anyone else while you work. Read the [privacy page](/privacy/) for the full detail, or open your browser's network tab and see for yourself.
