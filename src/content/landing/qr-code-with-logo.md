A logo in the middle of a QR code makes it look like it belongs to you, and tells people who it comes from before they scan. Done well, it costs nothing in reliability. Done badly, the code fails on half the phones you try. Here is how to get it right.

## How to add one

1. Create your code above first, using the URL, WiFi or other tab you need.
2. Use the "Upload logo" control to choose your image. It appears in the centre at once.
3. Adjust the size with the slider. The studio keeps it within a safe limit and raises the error correction level when needed.
4. Look at the scan badge. If it shows a warning, make the logo smaller or simplify it.
5. Download as SVG for print.

## Why it still scans

QR codes have built-in error correction: extra data that lets a reader rebuild the content even when part of the square is damaged or covered. There are four levels, from L (about 7% recoverable) to H (about 30%). A logo is just deliberate damage in a known place. At level H a logo covering a modest part of the centre leaves plenty of spare capacity. The studio raises the level automatically and tells you when it does.

## Rules of thumb

- Keep the logo to about a fifth of the code width or less. Past a quarter, scanning becomes unreliable.
- Use a logo with a solid background or clear outline so it stays distinct from the dots.
- Longer content means a denser code and less room for a logo. A short address leaves more space than a long contact card.
- Never cover the three large corner squares. They help cameras find the code.
- Test the printed result on several phones, in poor light and at the distance people will really scan from.

## Brand colours

You can colour the dots to match your brand. Keep dark dots on a light background, because the reverse is read poorly by some cameras. The scan badge checks contrast as well as the logo.

## Private by design

The logo is read in your browser and drawn into the code on your own device. It is not uploaded or stored on any server.
