A vCard QR code puts your contact details in a square. A person scans it and their phone offers to save you as a new contact, with your name, number, email and company already filled in. It is the modern replacement for typing a business card into a phone by hand.

## How to make one

1. Fill in your name and at least one way to reach you. Add organisation and job title if relevant.
2. Add a mobile number, a work number and an email. Leave out anything you do not want strangers to have.
3. Look at the preview. Each extra field makes the pattern denser, and the scan badge warns you if the code is getting too crowded to read well.
4. Download an SVG for print or a PNG for a screen or email signature.

## Where to use it

The back of a business card is the classic spot, and it saves you carrying two designs. Conference badges, email signatures, stall banners, shop counters and portfolio pages all work too. Trade show organisers like it because visitors scan once and the contact is saved without any typing.

## Be careful what you share

A code on a public poster can be read by anybody who passes, and they can keep your details. Use a work number and email for public places, and keep your personal mobile for people you give a card to in person. Once printed, a code cannot be recalled.

## Why it is static

The contact is stored inside the pattern itself. No server is involved, so there is no link that can break, no scan counter and no plan to renew. If your details change, print a fresh card. ## Private by design

The studio builds the code on your device. Your name, number and address are never uploaded, and the page asks nothing of other websites. Saved settings are kept in your browser only, and the Reset button clears them. See the [privacy page](/privacy/) for more.

## Getting a clean scan

Keep the border around the code, avoid low-contrast colours, and print it at least two centimetres wide, larger for posters. If you add a logo, keep it small, because it hides part of the pattern. Check the result with a few different phones before you print a large batch, as cameras vary more than you would think.
