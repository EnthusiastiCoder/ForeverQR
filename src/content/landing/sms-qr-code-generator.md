An SMS QR code opens a text message already addressed to your number, with your chosen words in the box. It suits anything where you want people to message you with a keyword: a raffle entry, a table number, a "yes, book me in" reply.

## How to make one

1. Enter the phone number, with the country code if people might scan from abroad.
2. Type the message that should appear in the box, such as "JOIN" or "Table 7, two coffees please".
3. Check the scan badge in the preview, then download as SVG or PNG.

## Where it helps

Shops that take orders by message, tutors who want a quick enquiry, community groups collecting names, and restaurants that text customers when a table is ready can all use it. It is quicker than reading out a number, and the message arrives in a format you can read at a glance, because you wrote the keyword.

## What it does not do

It does not send anything on its own. The person scanning still reviews the message and presses send, which is how it should be. Standard text rates apply, and some messaging apps handle a pre-filled body differently from classic SMS. Test it with a couple of phones, including one on each platform you care about.

It also does not count scans. We never see who scans the code, which keeps it private but means you will only know about people who actually send a message.

## Static, so no expiry

The number and message are inside the pattern, in the standard SMSTO format. No website sits between the person and your phone, so the code works as long as the number does. If you change numbers, make a new code.

## Printing advice

Keep the message short so the pattern stays simple and scans quickly. Use dark dots on a light background, leave the border intact, and print at least two centimetres across. Put a few words beside the code, such as "Scan to text us", so people know what will happen.

## Privacy

Your number and message are turned into the code in your browser, and nothing is uploaded. The site loads its fonts and scripts from itself, with no analytics.
